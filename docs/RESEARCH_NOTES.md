# Research notes

These original papers guide the CPU reference, experiments, and future work. The published poker systems are **not reproduced**. The table distinguishes ideas implemented in this restricted project from ideas still absent.

| Source | Core contribution and relevant location | Used here | Departure / pending work |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Zinkevich et al., 2007](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2007/file/08d98638c6fcd194a4b1e6992063e944-Paper.pdf) | Section 3, Equations 6–8: counterfactual regret weights alternatives by opponent/chance reach; regret matching normalizes positive cumulative regret. | New synchronous Kuhn/Leduc and tiny-river CFR references, with exact small-game or local best-response checks. | Restricted games and tiny explicit river ranges, not unabstracted full Hold'em. |
| [Heinrich and Silver, 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01121) | Algorithm 1 and Section 4.1: anticipatory mixing, replay-based approximate best response, reservoir-supervised average policy. | Historical Leduc NFSP and new separate full-hand restricted Hold'em NFSP, both with Q/target and policy networks and legal masks. | Different networks, optimizer, memories, action abstraction, and training horizon; Leduc NashConv and Hold'em fixed-opponent tests remain far from a full reproduction. |
| [Moravčík et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724) | Pages 4–8: continual re-solving needs own range and opponent counterfactual-value bounds; a depth-limited lookahead uses range-dependent value vectors. Figure 3 gives the network's pot/board/range inputs. | Tiny river local CFR and an exploratory hand-dependent conditional root-value model test the cost/accuracy tradeoff. | No depth cutoff, safe opponent-value constraints, continual re-solving, or DeepStack guarantee. |
| [Brown and Sandholm, Libratus](https://noambrown.com/papers/17-Science-Superhuman.pdf) | Blueprint abstraction, nested subgame solving, and self-improvement are distinct components. | Supports keeping offline learning and local solving as separate modules. | No Libratus blueprint, nested safe solving, or self-improver exists here. |
| [Brown et al., ReBeL, 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13544) | Section 4 introduces public belief states: public observations plus distributions over each player's private states, with search actions defined over private-state-contingent behavior. | The river prototype explicitly stores ranges and public action history. | No ReBeL value/policy training or learned belief-state search. |
| [Brown and Sandholm, Safe and Nested Subgame Solving, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02955) | Opponent value constraints in safe subgame solving. | Flags a safety condition for future continual re-solving. | No safe subgame solver exists yet. |

At a future CFR information set `I`, the intended regret increment for action `a` is `π₋ᵢ(I) π_c(I) [vᵢ(I,a) − vᵢ(I)]`; average strategy weight uses the acting player's reach `πᵢ(I)`. The CPU implementation must test chance weighting and the information-set grouping explicitly before interpreting its strategies.

The new [Kuhn reference](../small_games/kuhn_reference.py) and Leduc `SynchronousCFR` apply this reach weighting with strategies frozen for each complete deal sweep. Kuhn's first-player equilibrium value is −1/18 under the one-chip ante and one-chip bet rules; an [academic treatment](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/bard.msc.pdf) records that value. Exact best responses enumerate pure information-set policies in Kuhn and group hidden deals at the same information set in Leduc. Measured NashConv falls as CFR iterates.

The river prototype applies the same idea to a tiny explicit weighted range over real 52-card hands. In each information set the solver groups all compatible opponent hands sharing the acting player's private hand and public action history. Its hand-dependent label is currently a **conditional root EV** under the average river policy. With the generated disjoint ranges and normalized independent weights, this coincides with a root counterfactual value up to chance normalization; with card collisions or nonuniform own-hand reach, that distinction matters. The model is not trained on public betting states and has not been used at depth cutoffs. DeepStack-style opponent counterfactual-value constraints are absent, so no safe re-solving guarantee is claimed.

The [NFSP paper's Algorithm 1 and Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01121) motivated the separate Q and average-policy networks, reservoir, and anticipatory mode. The original Leduc experiment used a one-layer 64-unit network, SGD, larger memories, target updates every 300 updates, η=0.1, and decaying exploration. This project currently uses two 128-unit layers, Adam at 0.001, 10,000-entry memories, target updates every 200 updates, and fixed ε=0.1. Its custom Leduc rules also differ. We experimentally changed only η from 0.1 to 0.3 at 20,000 episodes and observed lower NashConv in three seeds; this is a local result, not a reproduction of the paper's reported equilibrium quality.

The separate 20-chip Hold'em NFSP path uses an 11-slot masked action abstraction, two 128-unit layers, Adam at 0.001, 100,000-entry replay and reservoir capacities, η=0.3, fixed ε=0.1, and 10,000 self-play hands per seed. Its observed return improved versus an initialized policy against random play across three seeds, but it lost chips to the heuristic. This establishes a working self-play learner under these rules, not an approximate Nash equilibrium. The local river CFR strategy is not a teacher for this NFSP network, and neither shares a value model.

GPU-CFR literature is still under review. Existing repository CUDA snippets are preserved but are not cited as research evidence or treated as validated accelerators. Original parallel-CFR papers must be verified before they inform a CUDA design or appear in the benchmark claims.
